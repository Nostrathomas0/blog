# How Kids Learn and How To Learn Like Them

Nobody remembers learning their first language. We don't remember first recognizing that *mom* is our favorite lady, *water* is the wet stuff that satisfies, and if you call someone's name, they answer. Every child developed their first language in a similar way. We acquired it from our families and our culture. It was just a part of our world and learning to speak was like learning to walk, a major node on our personal skilltree. To walk you need balance, to speak you need to hear and make sounds and you did this because you are human. Our brains and voices start us immediately to ascend the ladder of language and we keep learning well into our lives but it's those formative years that make our first language special. 

When an adult learns a second language there are major difference. Adults aren't provided with basic language education. We usually don't have long periods of play and freetime to develop our unique minds. Some of us don't even get enough sleep to give our brains time to self-organize. All is not lost at adulthood however, to a certin extent, we can recreate and even improve upon some of the conditions that we learn new languages under. The major differences between childhood language learning and adult language learning is neuroanatomical. The developing brain is uniquely suited to mimic and save speech paterns in the brain's deep storage. The adult brain has an easier time learning new skills and disciplines through the lens of our first language.
