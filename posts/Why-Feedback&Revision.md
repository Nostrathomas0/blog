\#Why You Keep Making the Same L2 Mistakes and How Feedback Helps



Unless you were lucky enough to be raised bilingual, you probably make mistakes when writing or speaking a second language. Even if you're tri-lingual you might still mistake verb for a noun or confuse their position in the sentence. Here's the secret to learning intricacies of a new language at the same level; study and speak with the curiosity of new learner.

When you learned how to use the faucet when you were thirsty, you took a basic need into your own hands. As a child you learned to set the table when your parents weren't there and you fed yourself. You can now meet your language needs with the same earnest focus. At it's base, any language is minutiae, blocks with which you express yourself. It's at this level that everything's constructed. You, even just by reading this, are creating thoughts with language. The next step is to create language with thought. 

It stands to reason that language learning is little more than reciprocal input and output. Different reading materials complement eachother and keeping a trans*l*ation dictionary between your L1 and L2 alongside a novel, article or blog is one of the first steps to retaining vocabulary. We should practice language like sports and love the endorphin rush of using new vocabulary, raising up from that B1 plateau.
