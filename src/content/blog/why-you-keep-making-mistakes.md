Unless you were lucky enough to be raised bilingual, you probably make mistakes when writing or speaking a second language — and even trilingual speakers still mix up a verb for a noun, or misplace it in the sentence. Here's the real secret to progressing past the mistakes you keep repeating: study and speak with the curiosity of a total beginner again.

## Mistakes are just unfinished feedback loops

Think back to learning how to use the tap when you were thirsty as a child, or setting the table when your parents weren't around. You took a basic need into your own hands and figured it out. You can meet your language needs with that same earnest, hands-on focus now.

At its base, a language is just minutiae — small blocks you use to express yourself. Everything is constructed at that level. You, right now, reading this, are creating thoughts *with* language. The next step — the one that actually eliminates repeated mistakes — is learning to create language *with* thought, deliberately, instead of on autopilot.

## Why feedback is the missing piece

Language learning is, at its core, reciprocal input and output. Different reading materials complement each other, and keeping a translation reference between your L1 and L2 next to a novel, article, or blog post is one of the first real steps toward retaining vocabulary — and toward noticing your own patterns of error.

We should treat language practice like sport: with the same appetite for the small endorphin rush of using a new word correctly, and the same tolerance for the repetition it takes to climb out of a plateau — especially that stubborn B1 plateau so many learners get stuck at.
