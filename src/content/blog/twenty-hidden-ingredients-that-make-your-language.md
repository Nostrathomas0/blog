### What's happening under the hood of your language?

Think of this list as the minituae of your language. These features are second nature to experts and thus, extremely difficult to acquire. There's real heavy lifting going on in the developing minds of children, and you don't remember learning it.

1. Isochrony - The arch difference between Germanic and Romance Languages, and the bane of learners across language families. Simply put, it describes how phrases stress is assigned and how it influences speech timing. Romance languages are **Syllable-timed** with each syllable getting roughly the same duration while Germanic languages are **Stress-timed** where only *stressed* syllables get the beat. Super difficult to learn.
2. Phrasal Intonation Contours - The rising and falling intonation of phrases and sentences that can emphasize words, signify sarcasm, questioning or other states. Often responsible for certain accents sounding angry or bored. Each language does this drastically different but most languages agree on a few topics like the rising intonation at the end of a question.
3. Suprasegmantals - The pitch, tone, stress and duration in words. Also known as prosody, this is the parts of a word apart from its articulation. English lexical stress can change meaning; *They re-CORD a RE-cord* Chinese marks every syllable with a flat, rising, falling or dipping tone;     *tāng **Soup** | táng **Candy** | tǎng **To lie down** | tàng **To scald**. Arabic kata-ba كَتَبَ means **He wrote** but holding the first vowel for a longer duration كَاتَبَ means **He corresponded with**. This also covers reduced vowels in unstressed syllables.
4. Allophonic Variation - Some sounds change a little bit depending on where they're spoken in a word or phrase. The letter T comes out in at least 5 different ways in the words, Tap, sTop, baT, baTTer or banTer. While you might consider all of these the same letter and perhaps even the same sound, your tongue has five different ways of pronouncing each one. Not that hard to learn, because it follows the physical articulatory methods that come with our speech muscles.
5. Phonotactic - This is the way people actually talk, complete with how we change words when chaining one word to the next.
